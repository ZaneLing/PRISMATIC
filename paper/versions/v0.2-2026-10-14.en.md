# PRISMATIC 2: A preliminary study of reviewable prescribing support and case growth

**Version v0.2 · 2026-10-14 · Research prototype · [中文](v0.2-2026-10-14.zh.md) · [Version index](../README.md)**

**Primary table: §4.1, Table 2. Primary dataset: a fixed 60-question public RxSafeBench sample.** This table tests three-option medication safety choice in simulated consultations. It does not test complete prescriptions, dose selection, or patient outcomes.

## Abstract

The 2025 PRISMATIC study proposed multi-agent prescription risk inspection. Whether accumulating cases improves prediction of supportive orders and whether a review harness improves medication safety require separate tests. We built a time-frozen, patient-disjoint MIMIC-IV/ED historical-order task and a paired public RxSafeBench harness pilot. At 40,000 training cases, an incremental model reached 0.4995 macro average precision across three supportive-order labels, versus 0.3683 at 250 cases; this measures prediction of recorded decisions. On 60 simulated safety-choice questions, we compared a direct answer, an unstructured second review, and an evidence-constrained bounded revision, using the same three models and the same initial answer. The harness changed the number correct by 0, −5, and +3 across the models, and every paired interval included zero (Table 2). We have not established that the system generates clinically appropriate complete prescriptions.

## 1. Introduction and research questions

We separate three claims. **H1:** more cases improve historical prediction of saline, antiemetic, and bowel-regimen orders. **H2:** on identical public questions, a constrained two-call review outperforms a direct answer and a budget-matched ordinary second review. **H3:** in blinded prescription-level clinical review, the system reduces serious medication errors without raising false alarms. This version pilots H1 and H2. H3 remains untested.

The proposed full architecture is a time-frozen chart snapshot, primary and supportive treatment candidates, retrieval of similar cases and adjudicated reasons, independent drug knowledge and rule checks, bounded revision or abstention, clinical feedback, and offline version promotion. The current `harness.py` implements a structured order-review interface with synthetic-rule tests. Table 2 evaluates a **lightweight evidence-review scaffold** applicable to public multiple-choice questions; it does not validate the full prescribing harness.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Paired comparison and operational harness definition

For every D1 question and model, we reuse the archived direct answer. Both second-pass arms **share that initial answer**. The ordinary review reconsiders it and returns A/B/C. The lightweight harness returns a verbatim dialogue excerpt, a medication concern, a KEEP/CHANGE decision, and a final option. A changed answer is rejected and the initial answer retained if the excerpt is absent from the dialogue or the JSON contract is invalid. This gate verifies **quote presence and revision bounds**, not medical correctness. The follow-up prompts contain no gold answer, correct-drug field, or annotation rationale.

Models were `openai/gpt-6.1-sol`, `google/gemini-3.8-flash`, and `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash`. All calls used temperature 0 and OpenRouter `zdr=true`, `data_collection=deny`. DeepSeek reasoning was explicitly disabled; the other providers used their defaults. Each second-pass arm adds one model call, giving equal call counts, though prompt lengths and actual token consumption differ. We report cost separately. Paired differences use 10,000 bootstrap resamples of questions with a fixed seed. Intervals are exploratory and not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Both formal second-call arms use `max_tokens=4096` with 18 concurrent workers; the archived first direct call used `max_tokens=128`. We therefore match **call count**, not token consumption. Development runs truncated by smaller limits are excluded from Table 2.

### 3.2 Case-growth task

D3 includes hospitalizations with at least one oral GSN order in the six hours after admission. Inputs are frozen at admission. Targets are IV isotonic saline, ondansetron, and senna/docusate orders in the next six hours. Training cases accumulate at 250, 1,000, 4,000, 16,000, and 40,000; patients do not cross train, validation, and test. We compare prevalence, 30-nearest-neighbor case memory, and incremental logistic SGD. A recorded order and a clinically appropriate order are distinct labels.

**Interpretation:** Relative to direct answers, the harness tied on GPT (35→35), lost five on Gemini (35→30), and gained three on DeepSeek (29→32); all three paired intervals include zero. Ordinary second review reduced accuracy for all three models. The constrained harness has higher point estimates than ordinary review, but the intervals include zero or touch its boundary. On DeepSeek, it corrected 10 questions and harmed 7, showing why final accuracy alone hides adverse revisions. **This pilot provides no evidence of a stable harness improvement over direct answering.**

## 5. Discussion, gaps, and next release

Table 2 concerns a lightweight public-MCQ review. It has not been joined to the structured order-rule interface in `harness.py` on a shared clinical prescription set. D1 is small and simulated, tests only the correct medication option, and includes two dialogues that directly mention the correct medication. D3 saline labels may include unnamed carrier uses and lack dose, infusion rate, indication, and clinician rationale. Historical matching cannot establish clinical safety or understanding of physician reasoning.

**The next release needs a prescription-level cross-method main table:** reproduce GAMENet, SafeDrug, and the available SafeRx-Agent implementation under common MIMIC preprocessing, patient splits, and ATC-L4 or RxNorm targets, and compare direct models, case retrieval, and the full harness under matched budgets. Mark unavailable implementations as independent reproductions. Add blinded dual-clinician review of supportive-order indications, saline purpose/volume/rate, omissions, false alerts, interactions, and renal adjustments. The complete RxSafeBench and RxQA releases can be secondary public sets; MedAgentBench can probe tools and state changes. Original scores from different papers do not enter Table 2.

## 6. Ethics, data boundary, and reproducibility

Patient-level MIMIC data, trained models, and predictions stay in the credentialed local directory. Following [PhysioNet guidance for third-party LLM services](https://physionet.org/news/post/llm-responsible-use/), this release sends only public RxSafeBench/RxQA questions to OpenRouter. The API key enters through a process environment variable and is not committed. These results are not for clinical prescribing.
