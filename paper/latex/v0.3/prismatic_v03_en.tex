\title{RxRSIAgent: Tracing Errors and Testing Scoped Updates\\for Prescription Assistance}
\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{abstract}
Prescription agents need patient-applicable evidence and experience updates that transfer beyond their training cases. We introduce RxRSIAgent, a three-call workflow with label retrieval, source-bound checks and a trace--reflection--intervention loop. A development gate controls updates to scoped procedures and bounded tool configurations. We distinguish accepted state changes from researcher repairs and supervised calibration. The frozen agent answers 491/988 RxSafeBench and 196/300 public RxQA questions. It does not exceed the strongest completed controls on both tasks. The QA state retains 0 procedural cards. We also test 467 independently sourced medication-safety questions and probe patient-context and option-position sensitivity. A local catalogue-constrained adapter is evaluated on 1,082 MIMIC-IV admissions: drug F1 changes from 7.19\% to 7.19\% after development retains 0 conditional update(s). A separate 72-case updater pilot retains 2 skills and changes drug F1 from 7.21\% to 16.96\% on 48 historically exposed audit admissions. A uniform complete-array decoding replay yields 16.10\% drug F1 for the same retained state; this engineering change is not RSI. We report rejected updates and regressions as well as corrections. The experiments measure reference agreement and update reliability, not clinical prescribing effectiveness or autonomous improvement of model weights.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Medication recommendation uses patient information to support drug selection and management. A systematic survey identifies temporal history, personalization and drug--drug interactions as recurring challenges \citep{mrsurvey}. Language-model approaches extend this task to clinical narratives, external evidence and verification \citep{lamrec,saferx}. Deployment is entering bounded prescribing workflows: Utah announced the Doctronic prescription-renewal pilot in January 2026 \citep{utahrenewal}. As of October 16, the official registry still places Doctronic in Phase 1, with practitioner authorization for every request. The Nolla Health adult topical-acne pilot began on October 5,2026 and permits specified initial prescriptions and refills, initially reviewed by two physicians \citep{utahpilots}. These scoped programs do not confer unrestricted autonomous prescribing authority \citep{utahfaq}. They motivate evaluation of accurate, comprehensive and adaptable treatment decisions; authorization alone does not establish effectiveness.

Existing benchmarks cover different portions of prescribing. MIMIC-based medication-set studies use cohort-specific drug spaces \citep{gamenet,armr,mimiciv}. RxSafeBench uses three-option medication-safety questions derived from simulated consultations \citep{rxsafe}. RxQA tests medication reasoning grounded in formulary information \citep{rxqa}; RxEval evaluates medication--dose--route choices, while Rx-LLM separates management tasks including orders, interactions and renal dosing \citep{rxeval,rxllm}. RxBench studies prescription review, and MedAgentBench tests tool execution in an interactive EHR \citep{rxbench,medagentbench}. MedPIC-Bench changes patient information to probe medication-safety reasoning \citep{medpic}. These resources do not share a single endpoint or directly comparable published score.

Methods address complementary problems. GAMENet uses medication graphs and patient history; SafeDrug uses molecular representations and interaction objectives; ARMR models changing treatment needs \citep{gamenet,safedrug,armr}. Language-assisted recommendation addresses alignment and overprescription, while FLAME optimizes list-wise medication generation with step-wise reinforcement learning \citep{lamrec,flame}. SafeRx-Agent combines specialty and supportive-care experts with retrieval and verification; RareAgents studies multidisciplinary diagnosis and medication recommendation for rare diseases \citep{saferx,rareagents}. MedAgents and MDAgents use expert collaboration \citep{medagents,mdagents}. Self-Refine revises outputs, Reflexion stores linguistic feedback, GRASP gates persistent skills against regressions, and SkeMex learns skill utility and manages a reusable repository \citep{selfrefine,reflexion,grasp,skemex}. These mechanisms motivate experience-based prescription agents, but a stored reflection or a larger memory does not itself demonstrate transferable improvement.

Our error analysis identifies three obstacles. Retrieved text may describe the wrong product or a condition absent in the patient; broadly routed skills can break previously correct decisions; and numerical gains can arise from medication-identity statistics. \textbf{The central problem is to make prescribing agents more accurate and complete for the observed patient, by converting traceable reference discrepancies into narrowly applicable process improvements that survive counterexamples and transfer to independent cases.} A successful update must do more than restate the answer or match a medication prior. Historical prescriptions record clinician actions rather than their full reasoning or all acceptable alternatives. Medicines optimization requires patient-specific review \citep{nice,who}; reference agreement, clinical need coverage and update reliability therefore require distinct evaluation.

We propose \textbf{RxRSIAgent}. It records the observed discrepancy, the earliest suspected failure stage, source and patient evidence, the proposed repair, and the repair's actual corrections and regressions. Its tools distinguish provenance from applicability; its task-specific learners can modify procedural memory and bounded tool settings. Backbone weights and executable tool code remain fixed within a trajectory. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An inspectable prescribing-assistance workflow} connecting product variants, evidence spans and narrow executable checks to explicit unresolved conditions.
\item \textbf{A trace-based update mechanism} with observable routing, counterexamples, persistent rejected-intervention feedback, and separately measured state changes.
\item \textbf{A falsifiable evaluation} of choice reasoning and decision-time structured order prediction, with named-agent task adaptations, a released FLAME policy component, frozen trajectories, cross-backbone transfer and shortcut diagnostics.
\end{itemize}

\section{RxRSIAgent}
\subsection{Evidence and decision workflow}
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} shows the public-QA workflow. Its served backbone is \texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash}. Each case permits three calls, each with a 1,536-token output cap. These outputs are benchmark option selections. A separate local task adapter selects structured orders from a common catalogue (Section~\ref{sec:local-orders}); restricted EHR records never enter the hosted model. Neither adapter submits a clinical order.

\paragraph{Extract observable context.}
Call 1 identifies question intent, quoted patient facts and medication entities. Patient facts require exact input spans. Routes are retained only when the quoted span explicitly supports a recognized route. Missing information remains unknown. Medication mentions in both the question and options are considered, with current treatment distinguished from proposed alternatives.

\paragraph{Retrieve product-specific evidence.}
The tool resolves brands, ingredients and salt names against frozen labels and cached openFDA requests \citep{openfda}. A single ingredient cannot silently inherit an unrelated combination-product label. It exposes available routes and product metadata, rejecting excerpts with an explicit route conflict. Up to two distinct route/release-form signatures are retrieved by default. Form metadata may be missing and is not a complete strength/form compatibility verifier.

TF--IDF ranks source windows using the question and extracted focus. Indication, dose and safety slots are reserved per product. Round-robin allocation distributes an 18,000-character default budget across products; each excerpt retains label ID, date, section, paragraph and offset. Default windows contain 1,600 characters. Scoped updates can change bounded retrieval parameters. Requests are cached, including HTTP failures, and subject to a declared query cap. Missing evidence remains unknown; source coverage does not establish relevance or safety.

\paragraph{Assess applicability and execute narrow checks.}
Call 2 evaluates every option, distinguishes contraindications from precautions, and proposes exact citations and supported arithmetic. The verifier checks source substrings and bounded numeric expressions. An added comparison tool binds an observed age, eGFR or creatinine-clearance value to an exact patient quote and an explicitly quoted source threshold. Unsupported parameters, operators or values remain unverified. Even a successful comparison does not verify units, all rule qualifiers or overall clinical appropriateness. Call 3 uses the original input, assessment and check outcomes to produce the final answer and uncertainty. Multi-answer questions require the complete answer set; a pre-test task adapter removes inherited single-answer JSON examples. Two incomplete RxSafe cases later required a declared deterministic list-to-text focus repair. The other 986 saved predictions were preserved byte-for-byte; this post-freeze engineering amendment is not an RSI gain.

\subsection{Trace reflection and scoped updates}
For the QA trajectory, let $A_t=(\theta,T,P,K_t,R_t,V_t)$ contain fixed backbone weights, executable tools and prompts, together with procedural cards, retrieval settings and condition vocabulary. These three latter components are the candidate learned state. No option-frequency model, raw case--answer retrieval or learned drug-name score overrides this QA actor. The local prescription adapter instead uses a fixed supervised candidate generator, and its bounded update state contains cards and verifier scope. The two task adapters do not share a learned memory or an identical development protocol.

\paragraph{Trace the gap before proposing a fix.}
Four rounds reveal 100 training cases each. For every newly observed error, the reflector receives the full input, reference, prediction, retrieved excerpts, intermediate assessment and check results. It reports an observed discrepancy, earliest suspected stage, causal hypothesis, exact supporting quotes, a minimal process change and a counterexample requirement. References may be ambiguous; this is a model-generated diagnosis, not clinical adjudication. Only quote-valid roots marked supported can sponsor a procedural proposal. Detector gaps compare proposed context features with the actually detected features.

\paragraph{Change one scoped behavior.}
Each candidate has one narrow observable trigger, optional exclusions, a procedure, supporting failures and successful counterexamples. At most one retrieval parameter may change. New context terms must be general normalized phrases without drug names or numeric patient values. A patch must match at least one supporting failure and one successful counterexample; this does not validate every term independently. The fixed matcher applies these terms before routing. This makes vocabulary a measurable tool-configuration update, not newly generated executable code. Applicable cards enter calls 2 and 3; inactive cards do not modify the prompts. Exact role and scope validation precedes inference. Schema repairs receive invalid IDs and legal pools. The final updater uses compact root-feature clusters, stable short IDs and typed failure/success pools. An external proposer, \texttt{anthropic/claude-opus-4.8}, receives at most three full training traces per proposal; the actor and initial root reflector retain the fixed DeepSeek backbone. This is externally assisted agent-state learning with additional training compute, not same-model autonomous recursion. All past quote-validated roots remain eligible through the current round, with originating state hashes preserved. Recent rejected edits and the two highest positive-net failed probes are replayed to later proposals. The incumbent is re-evaluated before each new probe. An explicit no-edit response is retained with its reason and is never forced into an unsupported procedure.

\paragraph{Gate the intervention.}
At most four candidates are tested per round. Each first runs on up to 32 accumulated training failures and successes, including its designated support and counterexamples. It must produce at least two net corrections with no extra invalid outputs. The proposal stage then requires no accuracy loss on either RxSafe 190 or MedQA 120 and a strictly positive pooled gain. The separate guard requires no loss on either RxSafe 197 or MedQA 120, with no increase in invalid answers. Rejection restores the incumbent. The final retained state is selected by this development rule, never by main-benchmark or independent-test scores. Earlier updater controls required a strictly positive pooled guard gain; the current non-regression guard was declared before independent evaluation and is disclosed as a protocol change.

\subsection{Why supervised calibration is a separate baseline}
The numerical comparator trains an ordinary logistic regression on 625 labeled RxSafe cases. Each candidate option is represented by normalized drug identity, drug/UNII--focus-word interactions and an LLM draft indicator. Development selects regularization $C=10$ and a probability-gap threshold of 0.2. The model may override the LLM only when all options are short, previously observed medication names. This learns statistical weights from GT; it does not trace a failure, invent a procedure or recursively improve a tool. Its previously recorded local result is retained as a \emph{supervised control}, not credited to RxRSIAgent's procedural evolution. A name-only diagnostic performs strongly, motivating independent patient-condition tests.

\subsection{Failure-directed repair}
In a public training iron-treatment question, retrieval returns a same-ingredient homeopathic product with an unrelated indication. Preserving product metadata and filtering its scope restores reference agreement for that case. Yet the population-level routed repair loses one correct answer on each proposal task and is not retained. Appendix~\ref{app:cases} gives the before/after evidence and additional regressions. A local order example exposes a different failure: expanding fact scope cannot help when the triggering case contains only one observed fact. Case repair and population generalization are separate requirements.


\subsection{Choice reasoning versus prescription prediction}
The public choice benchmarks have distinct endpoints: RxSafeBench is three-way choice, public RxQA is four-way choice, and MedPIC includes single- and multiple-answer items. Their accuracies are not prescription coverage. For a predicted drug set $P$ and recorded reference $G$, recall is $|P\cap G|/|G|$ and precision is $|P\cap G|/|P|$. Both accompany F1, Jaccard, omissions, extras and prescription size. Core4 matches drug, route, dose and unit jointly; Core5 additionally matches frequency. Tuple agreement uses complete observed labels and is reported with field completeness. An extra relative to recorded treatment is not automatically clinically inappropriate. The following local evaluation predicts the full recorded target set within a fixed six-hour window; it does not infer the entire hospitalization regimen.



\subsection{Local structured-prescription evaluation}\label{sec:local-orders}
We extend the workflow to the entire set of new/change orders within the audited six-hour window. The cohort is retrospectively restricted to nonempty targets, complete 12-hour follow-up and no ambiguous target rows. Provider order and pharmacy entry times must both fall in hours 6--12, with verification by hour 12. Prior orders and labs are filtered by recorded availability clocks. Released field-version histories are incomplete; this does not establish all-admission performance. This is a local prescription-task adaptation of RxRSIAgent; the hosted QA backbone is not sent restricted EHR records. A frozen 40,000-admission supervised tool proposes 24 joint order templates from the original 128-name vocabulary, shared by all local actors. It supplies a candidate space rather than overriding the final selection. Every out-of-vocabulary target remains in recall. Thus this is catalogue-constrained structured order prediction, not unrestricted prescribing or prediction of the entire hospitalization. Facts are bound to observed input identifiers, product-label excerpts are retrieved offline, and selected identifiers compile to joint drug/route/dose/unit/frequency fields. Missing information is preserved. Current diagnoses, allergy completeness, renal clearance, infusion purpose and duration are not established by this input contract.

\section{Clinical Scope and Limitations}
QA agreement does not measure complete treatment coverage, omissions, unnecessary medication, dose/route/schedule correctness, full interactions, organ-function adjustment or monitoring. The local order experiment adds set and joint-field agreement within a fixed catalogue and time window; it does not establish unrestricted prescribing competence. A separately specified clinician rubric follows medicines review and the Medication Appropriateness Index \citep{nice,mai}; two available reviewers have not returned blinded ratings. No clinical outcome is inferred. Complete author-workflow prescription comparisons and real MedAgentBench tool/state execution remain unfinished.

Repeated main-benchmark exposure, reused development cohorts, small local backbones and hosted-provider nondeterminism limit inference. A model's root explanation is a hypothesis, even when quotations are valid. Sparse or inconsistent feedback can yield unsupported procedures. Context terms may overgeneralize, and natural-language counterexamples are not formally validated clinical exclusions. Label coverage, exact provenance and numeric comparisons do not establish complete applicability; missing formulations, strengths, units and temporal treatment context remain limitations. Equal output allowance does not equal compute, information or supervision. The two local backbones test a limited transfer question, not model-independent benefit. This work studies feedback-guided agent-state updates; it does not establish autonomous recursive improvement of model weights or tool code.

\section{Conclusion}
RxRSIAgent connects accumulated errors to observable trace hypotheses, scoped interventions and explicit retention decisions. Ordinary supervised option calibration is kept separate. The QA trajectory does not demonstrate positive procedural evolution or superiority on both choice tasks. The expanded local updater is evaluated separately against static tools on a small exploratory audit; state changes alone do not establish transferable clinical learning. In the full-cohort restricted-interface study, 0 conditional update(s) are retained; the measured drug-F1 change is +0.00 points, with a 95\% interval [+0.00, +0.00].  Independent patient-conditioned evaluation and transparent failure accounting remain necessary before claiming progressively better prescribing.

\section*{Ethics and Reproducibility}
Only public benchmark material is sent to the external provider in these experiments. Restricted clinical records, credentials, raw responses and downloaded label bodies remain local. Public artifacts include source pins, code, aggregate results, state hashes and rejected gates. Source and response receipts support independent recounts. These research outputs are not autonomous clinical prescriptions.

\section{Failure Cases and Population Checks}\label{app:cases}

\paragraph{Product identity is insufficient.}
In training case\texttt{e7a30d739ee5b9be}, the reference is ferrous sulfate, but the actor chooses ferrous fumarate. Retrieval supplies a same-ingredient homeopathic product whose indication concerns hot flashes; the original reader discards the source field declaring its homeopathic status. Full metadata and product-scope filtering restore reference agreement. OTC status alone must not trigger this exclusion, and listing in openFDA does not establish approval. This repairs an evidence mismatch, not proof that every alternative iron treatment is inappropriate.

\paragraph{A correct source can lose its decisive condition.}
A heart-failure combination question has isosorbide dinitrate/hydralazine as its reference, whereas the actor chooses amlodipine/valsartan. Component-only retrieval misses regimen-level evidence. Retrieving the combination label still fails when a character window removes the population qualifier. Expanding to the complete original sentence restores reference agreement in this example. The source also specifies adjunctive treatment; its population qualifier is not a sufficient standalone prescribing rule.

\paragraph{Some premises survive explicit verification prompts.}
In\texttt{20084f0b868433c8}, the actor infers dialysis from recurring transfusions and uses it to exclude the reference treatment. Product retrieval changes the answer to the reference, but the additional decision-obligation prompt still selects the original alternative. In another training case, the actor confuses intervention during active diphtheria with pre-exposure toxoid vaccination; all tested repairs remain incorrect. Source-provenance checks do not validate semantic entailment, and label retrieval does not replace treatment-phase guidance.

\paragraph{From case repair to population validation.}
Across 400 training traces, structural checks identify 8 cases with a shared component across options and 10 requiring joint-regimen retrieval; each group contains 2 original errors. These patterns account for only part of the 131 observed failures. The repaired tools preserve option roles, full product metadata and combination identities. A further candidate expands evidence to whole sentences under the same 18,000-character source limit. A final router activates only for source-classified homeopathic products or joint-regimen options, preserving the original evidence and requests elsewhere. No answer label or prediction correctness is read by the router. Its design follows observed development failures and is not an unseen-test intervention.
